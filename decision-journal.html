Decision 042 · March 2026

Horizon Growth Partners V

Passed
Proposed commitment$2,000,000
Decision horizon30 days
Confidence at decision72%
Original thesis

An experienced manager with a repeatable sourcing advantage, attractive sector exposure, and a credible opportunity to compound capital over a ten-year horizon.

Portfolio context

Private equity already represented 18% of family capital, with $5.9 million of unfunded commitments and meaningful operating-business concentration.

Alternatives considered

Commit half the proposed amount, wait for the next vintage, or redirect capital toward liquid public markets and shorter-duration private credit.

Risks identified

Manager turnover, valuation discipline, overlapping sector exposure, and a liquidity profile that became less attractive when viewed across the full portfolio.

Decision

Pass. The fund may be good, but the commitment is not right for the family at this point in the portfolio cycle.

Expected outcome

The manager will likely perform reasonably well. The family expects to preserve flexibility and avoid raising private-market exposure above its intended range.

Actual outcome

Two years later, the fund marked up early investments, but distributions remained limited. The family used the preserved liquidity for a more attractive direct opportunity.

Lesson carried forward

A strong manager is not automatically a strong portfolio decision. Evaluate opportunity quality and portfolio fit separately.

The discipline

Every meaningful decision follows the same sequence.

01 · Context

What was true at the time?

Portfolio exposure, liquidity, commitments, family goals, tax position, market conditions, and any personal or behavioral pressures affecting the choice.

02 · Thesis

Why might this work?

The core investment case, the source of expected return, assumptions that must hold, and evidence supporting the opportunity.

03 · Disconfirmation

What would prove us wrong?

The risks, contrary evidence, alternative interpretations, and specific developments that should cause the family to reconsider.

04 · Alternatives

What else could we do?

Invest less, wait, choose a different structure, preserve liquidity, sell something else, or decline the opportunity entirely.

05 · Decision

What did we choose—and why?

The final choice, level of conviction, decision owner, dissenting views, and the conditions under which the decision should be revisited.

06 · Postmortem

What did experience teach us?

Expected versus actual results, what was skill or luck, which assumptions mattered, and the lesson that should change future behavior.

The compounding effect

A portfolio compounds capital.
A journal compounds judgment.

Over time, the family builds a searchable record of repeated mistakes, durable strengths, decision patterns, manager experiences, and principles earned through actual choices.

Behavior

Recognize recurring mistakes

Overconfidence, urgency, familiarity bias, performance chasing, reluctance to sell, and decisions shaped by social proof become visible across time.

Managers

Compare promises with reality

Original underwriting, communication quality, portfolio construction, fees, capital deployment, and realized outcomes remain connected.

Family

Create shared institutional memory

Spouses, children, trustees, and future advisers can understand not just what happened, but what the family knew and believed when it decided.

What it prevents

Hindsight should inform the record.
It should not rewrite it.

Outcome bias

A winner is not always a good decision

The journal preserves whether the original thesis was thoughtful, risks were understood, and the decision fit the portfolio.

Selective memory

The passes remain visible

Declined opportunities, missed gains, avoided losses, and the reasons behind inaction become part of the family's learning history.

False certainty

Confidence can be compared with reality

Recording conviction at the time reveals where the family is well calibrated—and where confidence routinely exceeds understanding.
