Why it exists

Portfolios drift.
Stories harden.

Investments accumulate one decision at a time. Over years, the original rationale fades, inherited positions become permanent, and yesterday’s assumptions begin masquerading as enduring principles.

Memory

The original thesis fades

Families remember the outcome more clearly than the assumptions, alternatives, and risks that shaped the decision.

Inertia

Ownership becomes its own argument

A position remains because it is already there—not because the family would choose it again today.

Complexity

No one sees the whole portfolio

Managers, advisers, entities, and family members each understand a piece, while the combined risks remain obscured.

The question

If every position disappeared overnight,
which ones would you buy back tomorrow?

Conviction Review forces the family to separate deliberate ownership from accumulated ownership.

The review

Six questions. No performance theater.

01

What do we own?

A complete portfolio across public markets, private investments, real estate, businesses, cash, debt, trusts, and entities.

02

Why do we own it?

The purpose, thesis, time horizon, expected return, role in the portfolio, and reason the family chose it over alternatives.

03

What changed?

New information, family circumstances, valuations, manager changes, liquidity needs, tax assumptions, and shifts in the opportunity set.

04

What were we wrong about?

Assumptions that failed, risks that were missed, outcomes attributed to luck, and lessons that should alter future decisions.

05

Where is conviction weakest?

Positions the family would not initiate today, exposures nobody can defend clearly, and assets being held by default.

06

What happens next?

Specific actions, owners, deadlines, open questions, and decisions that must be revisited during the coming year.

The room

Challenge without judgment.
Accountability without performance ranking.

The member

Explains the portfolio

The member presents the actual rationale, uncertainty, mistakes, and decisions—not a polished defense prepared by an adviser.

The council

Tests the conviction

Peers ask questions, identify blind spots, share relevant experience, and challenge positions that survive mainly through inertia.

The adviser

Turns insight into action

The adviser connects the conversation to portfolio data, family priorities, tax consequences, liquidity, and a documented follow-through plan.

What it is not

No rankings. No grandstanding. No forced consensus.

Not a pitch

No one is selling the room

The review cannot be used to distribute products, solicit capital, or promote a manager, adviser, or transaction.

Not hindsight theater

Good outcomes can hide weak thinking

Results are examined alongside the original Decision Journal so luck is not confused with skill.

Not group control

The family still decides

The council improves the questions and exposes tradeoffs. It does not vote on the family’s portfolio.
