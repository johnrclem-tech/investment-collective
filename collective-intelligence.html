The Community

Relevant experience.
Right when you need it.

The Community helps a family find members who have already evaluated the manager, hired the adviser, used the structure, or faced the decision in front of them—then understand what those members learned before deciding what happens next.

Why Community Matters How Community Works

The problem

The right experience often exists.
The family just cannot reliably find it.

A member may already have evaluated the same manager, negotiated the same transaction, worked with the same specialist, or lived through the same family decision. That experience is valuable—but usually scattered across private relationships, isolated conversations, and memories that disappear over time.

01

Experience is fragmented

Useful lessons sit with individual families, advisers, institutions, and personal networks that are not connected.

02

Personal networks are incomplete

The member with the most relevant experience may sit well outside the family’s existing circle.

03

Reasoning and outcomes disappear

What was considered, why a decision was made, and what happened afterward are rarely preserved together.

The relationship

The Council knows the family.
The Community knows the question.

The Council provides recurring perspective from a small group that increasingly understands the family. The Community expands the search when a specific decision would benefit from experience beyond that room. The Advisor determines what is relevant and remains accountable for the recommendation.

What the Community preserves

Experience becomes useful when the reasoning survives the conversation.

The Community does not reduce member experience to a vote, rating, or list of names. It preserves enough context to understand what the member faced, how they evaluated it, what happened, and whether the lesson is relevant to another family.

The questionThe decision, manager, adviser, structure, transaction, or family issue under consideration.
Family contextRelevant portfolio, liquidity, ownership, tax, business, and family circumstances.
The thesisWhy the member considered the decision attractive, necessary, or worth exploring.
TradeoffsThe risks, opportunity costs, alternatives, and constraints that shaped the choice.
ConflictsOwnership, compensation, referrals, and economic relationships connected to the experience.
The outcomeWhat happened after the decision and which expectations proved right or wrong.
Lessons learnedWhat surprised the member and what they would repeat, change, or avoid.
PermissionHow the experience may be shared and whether a direct introduction is appropriate.

How Community works

Search is the beginning.
Understanding is the value.

The objective is not to count how many members invested, hired, or passed. It is to understand why experienced families reached different conclusions and determine what applies to the family making the decision now.

01

Ask a specific question

Define the manager, adviser, structure, investment, transaction, or family decision under consideration.

02

Surface relevant experience

Find documented decisions and members whose firsthand experience meaningfully matches the question.

03

Understand the reasoning

Review the thesis, tradeoffs, conflicts, sizing, alternatives, surprises, outcomes, and lessons learned.

04

Request a connection

When a direct conversation would add value, the contributing member decides whether to accept the introduction.

05

Apply the family’s context

The Advisor separates recurring signal from anecdote and connects the experience to the family’s own priorities and constraints.

06

Preserve what follows

The family’s reasoning, decision, outcome, and lessons become part of the Community only at the level it permits.

The Advisor’s role

The Community broadens the evidence. The Advisor decides what applies.

Member experience can improve the question, reveal patterns, and identify people worth speaking with. The Advisor remains responsible for evaluating relevance, identifying conflicts, connecting the evidence to the family’s complete context, and making an independent recommendation.

Separate recurring signal from isolated anecdotes Make conflicts and economic relationships visible Connect experience to allocation, liquidity, taxes, trusts, and family priorities Explain where another family’s context materially differs Make a clear recommendation and coordinate what happens next

Designed for trust

Private by default. Useful by permission.

The Community works only when members control how their experience is shared and the network never becomes a marketplace.

Member controlled

Members determine whether experience is identified, anonymous, limited, or shared only through an Advisor.

Permissioned connection

Introductions and direct conversations happen only with the contributing member’s approval.

No selling

No solicitation, pay-to-play visibility, placement economics, or compensation tied to member activity.
