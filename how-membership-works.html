The idea

The service should become useful before the next major decision arrives.

The first phase creates a complete operating picture. The ongoing experience keeps that picture current, brings the right people into the room, and preserves what the family learns.

Getting started

Three phases. One connected system.

01 · Understand

Map the family capital

Accounts, private investments, real estate, trusts, entities, liabilities, advisers, commitments, goals, and expected liquidity are organized into one family balance sheet.

Typical timing: weeks 1–4
02 · Prioritize

Identify what deserves attention

The first Allocation Insights surface concentration, liquidity pressure, commitment pacing, document gaps, and decisions that should not wait for a routine review.

Typical timing: month 2
03 · Activate

Establish the decision rhythm

The family begins quarterly reviews, joins a carefully matched Peer Council, and uses the full decision process when the next meaningful opportunity arrives.

Typical timing: month 3

What the family receives

Clarity first.
Then an ongoing operating relationship.

Investment Office

One connected view

A current picture of assets, ownership, liquidity, commitments, risks, advisers, documents, and approaching decisions.

Independent advice

Recommendations in context

Advice is evaluated against the entire family balance sheet—not one account, manager, product, or transaction.

Peer Council

A trusted decision room

Six to eight members meet around real opportunities, postmortems, family questions, and the Annual Allocation Review.

Decision system

A record that compounds

Allocation Insights, Collective Intelligence, and the Decision Journal improve each decision and preserve what experience teaches.

Who does what

The family remains the decision-maker.

The family

Sets priorities and decides

The family defines its goals, values, constraints, and final choices.

Investment Collective

Creates context and coordination

The Investment Office organizes the picture, frames the decision, coordinates advisers, and documents the reasoning.

The council

Improves the questions

Peers challenge assumptions and share relevant experience without voting, selling, or controlling the portfolio.

What membership requires

Participation matters.

The collective becomes valuable when members prepare, attend, share honestly, protect confidentiality, disclose conflicts, and contribute experience as generously as they receive it.
