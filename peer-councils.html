Peer Councils

A personal investment committee.
Built around trust.

Small, carefully assembled groups of experienced investors who help one another ask better questions, challenge assumptions, and make clearer decisions.

06–08Members per council

Why councils work

Different experience. Shared responsibility.

Every member brings a distinct background, pattern-recognition advantage, and body of lived experience. The value comes from the combination—not from everyone thinking alike.

Built for candor

Small by design

Six to eight members creates enough diversity for useful perspective without turning the room into an audience.

Built for relevance

Carefully assembled

Councils are matched around investment complexity, career stage, temperament, experience, and potential conflicts.

Built for contribution

Everyone participates

Members are expected to bring real decisions, real mistakes, and real lessons—not simply consume the experience of others.

Built for trust

Confidential by default

What is shared in the council stays in the council. Trust is treated as infrastructure, not etiquette.

Built for independence

No selling

No member, manager, adviser, or sponsor earns anything because another member invests.

Built for discipline

Decisions are documented

Questions, assumptions, risks, and lessons are captured so experience becomes reusable intelligence.

What it is not

Not networking.
Not fundraising.
Not performance theater.

A council is not a mastermind, social club, deal room, or stage for impressive opinions. It is a structured environment for better judgment.

Inside a meeting

Two hours. One real agenda.

01 · Check-in

What changed?

Each member shares the decision, concern, or change currently occupying their attention. The council sees the issues before they become emergencies.

02 · Member hot seat

One decision goes deep

A member presents the facts, choices, current inclination, risks, and the kind of perspective they need. The council asks questions before offering opinions.

03 · Postmortem

What actually happened?

A member revisits an investment that succeeded, disappointed, or was declined. The focus is not the outcome alone, but the quality of the original reasoning.

04 · Adviser synthesis

Turn discussion into context

The adviser separates useful peer experience from herd behavior, highlights missing information, and connects the discussion back to the member's complete financial situation.

05 · Commitments

What happens next?

The meeting ends with documented decisions, unanswered questions, assigned follow-up, and a clear record of what each member intends to do.

Council questions first

Questions before answers

What would have to be true?

What role should this investment play? What could force liquidity at the wrong time? Which existing exposure does it duplicate? What would make you regret the position size? What did comparable investors underestimate?

The goal is not to reach consensus. The goal is to improve the quality of the decision.

The signature experience

Portfolio Defense

Once each year, every member presents what they own, why they own it, what keeps them awake, what they got wrong, what changed, and what comes next.

01

My portfolio

The actual allocation across public markets, private investments, real estate, cash, trusts, entities, and concentrated positions.

02

Why I own it

The thesis, role, expected return, and reason each major exposure belongs in the portfolio.

03

What I got wrong

The assumptions that failed, risks that were missed, and decisions that would be made differently today.

04

What concerns me

The concentrations, liquidity needs, family changes, market risks, and future commitments requiring attention.

05

What changed

The new information, experiences, and life events that altered the member's thinking during the year.

06

What comes next

The decisions, reductions, commitments, and questions that form the coming year's investment agenda.

Council standards

Trust requires clear rules.

01Confidentiality

No recordings, screenshots, forwarding, or identification of another member's information.

02Conflict disclosure

Members disclose ownership, compensation, relationships, and other interests before discussing an opportunity.

03Non-solicitation

The council cannot be used to raise capital, sell professional services, or pressure members into opportunities.

04No transaction economics

No one is compensated based on whether another member completes an investment.

05Contribution

Members attend, prepare, share experience, and discuss failures as honestly as successes.

06Removal rights

Conduct that threatens trust, safety, or independence can result in immediate removal.

The standard

The strongest council is not the room with the most successful people.
It is the room where successful people can be honest.
