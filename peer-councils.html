The purpose

Investing at scale can be surprisingly isolating.

Friends may not share the same complexity. Advisers may see only part of the picture. A syndicateIQ council creates a durable place to learn from other families, test your own thinking, and follow through on decisions that are easy to postpone.

LearnHear how peers evaluate real choices, tradeoffs, mistakes, and outcomes.
PresentBring a difficult live decision into a structured room for candid feedback.
ActLeave with clearer questions, documented next steps, and accountability.

How councils are assembled

Councils are assembled.
Not filled.

Each council is deliberately built around useful differences in wealth origin, portfolio experience, family stage, decision style, communication style, and expertise. Members are not matched by net worth, geography, age, industry, politics, or social compatibility alone; judgment, discretion, contribution, and the quality of perspective around the table matter more.

Former operator

Private-market investor

Two decades evaluating funds

Family steward

Brings governance, trust, next-generation, family communication, and long-horizon capital experience.

Real-asset entrepreneur

Built wealth through property and credit

Brings leverage, illiquidity, underwriting, operating-partner, and downside-management experience.

Each quarter has an anchor gathering. Three are quarterly offsites; the fourth is the annual offsite. A virtual peer meeting is scheduled between each anchor so members remain connected to live decisions throughout the year.

What you get from the council

More than feedback.
Judgment that compounds.

Each gathering expands what you have seen, improves the decisions in front of you, and deepens relationships with people who increasingly understand your portfolio, your instincts, and your blind spots.

See more decisions

Learn from choices you did not have to make yourself

Hear how other families evaluate investments, liquidity, concentrated positions, advisers, leverage, trusts, business transitions, and next-generation questions—including what worked, what failed, and what surprised them.

Bring your own decision

Put the hardest question on the table

Present a live decision, clarify the real issue, test assumptions, hear relevant experiences, identify missing information, and consider alternatives you may not have seen alone.

Build pattern recognition

Recognize recurring risks earlier

Over time, repeated exposure to member decisions helps you spot manager issues, behavioral traps, liquidity pressure, governance problems, and family tradeoffs before they become expensive.

Create accountability

Turn important conversations into action

The council revisits unresolved issues, deferred decisions, and stated commitments—helping members follow through on changes that may otherwise remain permanently “under consideration.”

Access trusted experience

Reach people who have already lived through it

Members can offer firsthand perspective and thoughtful introductions to managers, lenders, attorneys, operators, advisers, or other families when a relationship would genuinely help.

Gain an enduring advisory board

Build relationships that get more useful over time

Your peers gradually understand how you decide, where you are strongest, what you repeatedly avoid, and which family or portfolio issues require more candor than a one-time conversation can provide.

Inside a virtual peer meeting

Focused enough for rigor.
Frequent enough to matter.

Regular peer meetings begin virtually so members can maintain a consistent decision rhythm regardless of geography. The format is designed for deep discussion without turning into another all-day obligation.

4:00 PM
Reconnect

Open the room

Members arrive, reconnect briefly, and align on the agenda and the decisions requiring attention.

4:15 PM
What changed

Brief updates from every member

Each member shares one meaningful decision, concern, portfolio change, or lesson since the last gathering.

4:35 PM
Decision room

One real decision goes deep

The presenting family explains the facts, choices, portfolio context, current inclination, risks, and the perspective it needs. Questions come before opinions.

5:35 PM
Postmortem

Revisit a decision already made

A member compares the original reasoning with what actually happened and identifies what the council should carry forward.

6:05 PM
Shared intelligence

Surface relevant experience

The facilitator brings forward a recurring pattern, relevant community experience, or an issue several families are facing.

6:20 PM
Commitments

Turn discussion into action

The group documents decisions, unanswered questions, requested introductions, follow-up owners, and review dates before closing at 6:30 PM.

The annual offsite

Annual Allocation Review

Once each year, the council meets in person for an extended offsite. Every family presents its complete allocation, liquidity, commitments, concentrations, unresolved risks, and changes in family priorities.

01

What still belongs?

Why does each major exposure continue to deserve capital, complexity, and attention?

02

What is no longer true?

Which original assumptions, relationships, or family circumstances have changed?

03

Where is confidence too high?

Which concentration, manager, asset, or family belief is not being challenged enough?

04

What happens next?

The family leaves with a documented allocation agenda, decisions to make, and issues to revisit.

Professionally facilitated

The facilitator is not the lecturer.

A syndicateIQ advisor builds the agenda, prepares the presenting member, keeps questions ahead of opinions, balances participation, surfaces conflicts, connects peer experience to family context, and documents follow-through.

Designed for trust

Candor requires clear rules.

ConfidentialityNothing leaves the room without permission.
No solicitationThe council cannot be used to raise capital or sell services.
Conflict disclosureEconomic interests and relevant relationships are stated first.
Experience, not prescriptionsMembers share what they learned—not instructions for what others should do.
ParticipationMembers attend, prepare, contribute, and discuss mistakes honestly.
Family decidesThe council provides perspective. The family retains control.
