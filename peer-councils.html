The purpose

Investing at scale can be surprisingly isolating.

Friends may not share the same complexity. Advisers may see only part of the picture. A syndicateIQ council creates a durable place to learn from other families, test your own thinking, and follow through on decisions that are easy to postpone.

What you get from the council

More than feedback.
Judgment that compounds.

Each gathering expands what you have seen, improves the decisions in front of you, and deepens relationships with people who increasingly understand your portfolio, your instincts, and your blind spots.

See more decisions

Learn from choices you did not have to make yourself

Hear how other families evaluate investments, liquidity, concentrated positions, advisers, leverage, trusts, business transitions, and next-generation questions—including what worked, what failed, and what surprised them.

Bring your own decision

Put the hardest question on the table

Present a live decision, clarify the real issue, test assumptions, hear relevant experiences, identify missing information, and consider alternatives you may not have seen alone.

Build pattern recognition

Recognize recurring risks earlier

Over time, repeated exposure to member decisions helps you spot manager issues, behavioral traps, liquidity pressure, governance problems, and family tradeoffs before they become expensive.

Create accountability

Turn important conversations into action

The council revisits unresolved issues, deferred decisions, and stated commitments—helping members follow through on changes that may otherwise remain permanently “under consideration.”

Access trusted experience

Reach people who have already lived through it

Members can offer firsthand perspective and thoughtful introductions to managers, lenders, attorneys, operators, advisers, or other families when a relationship would genuinely help.

Gain an enduring advisory board

Build relationships that get more useful over time

Your peers gradually understand how you decide, where you are strongest, what you repeatedly avoid, and which family or portfolio issues require more candor than a one-time conversation can provide.

Inside a monthly peer meeting

The work that makes
the meeting valuable.

Meetings are built around real member needs rather than a rigid agenda. These are the recurring forms of work that turn a group of experienced families into a useful personal investment council.

?

Issues & Opportunities — Hot Seat

One member brings a difficult live decision, opportunity, concern, or unresolved question to the council. The group clarifies the real issue, challenges assumptions, shares relevant experience, and identifies choices the member may not have considered.

↺

Peer Decisions & Postmortems

Members examine decisions already made—why a family invested, passed, sold, borrowed, changed a structure, or selected a strategy—and compare the original reasoning with what happened afterward.

✦

Subject Matter Experts

syndicateIQ brings in carefully selected specialists to address questions the council is actively facing, including estate planning, insurance, tax strategy, private investments, concentrated positions, and other technical areas.

◫

Annual Allocation Review

Once each year, every member presents the complete allocation, liquidity, commitments, concentrations, objectives, unresolved risks, and major changes for candid peer review.

◎

Collective Intelligence

A syndicateIQ advisor builds the agenda around live member needs, prepares the presenting member, brings relevant experience from the broader community into the room, keeps questions ahead of opinions, balances participation, surfaces conflicts, connects peer experience to the family’s portfolio context, and documents follow-through.

✓

Decisions, Introductions & Follow-Through

Important conclusions, unanswered questions, requested introductions, and next actions are documented so useful discussion leads to actual decisions and the council can revisit what happened.

Designed for trust

Candor requires clear rules.

ConfidentialityNothing leaves the room without permission.
No solicitationThe council cannot be used to raise capital or sell services.
Conflict disclosureEconomic interests and relevant relationships are stated first.
Experience, not prescriptionsMembers share what they learned—not instructions for what others should do.
ParticipationMembers attend, prepare, contribute, and discuss mistakes honestly.
Family decidesThe council provides perspective. The family retains control.
