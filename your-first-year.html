The experience

Membership becomes valuable when the pieces begin working together.

The Investment Office provides context. Allocation Insights identifies what deserves attention. The council improves the questions. Independent advice turns the discussion into a decision.

The first twelve months

Six milestones. One clearer way to decide.

Weeks 1–4

Understand the whole family

Accounts, private investments, real estate, trusts, entities, debt, advisers, commitments, goals, and expected liquidity are organized into one family balance sheet.

Month 2

See what deserves attention

The first Allocation Insights surface concentration, commitment pacing, liquidity pressure, and decisions that should not wait for a routine meeting.

Month 3

Meet the Peer Council

Twelve carefully matched members begin building the trust required to discuss real opportunities, mistakes, family questions, and difficult tradeoffs. The larger roster keeps the Council useful even when some members miss a session.

Explore Peer Councils
Month 4

Make the first decision differently

A new investment is evaluated through portfolio context, documented member experience, council questions, independent advice, and a clear final recommendation.

Quarterly

Review what changed

The agenda begins with new risks, approaching decisions, liquidity, follow-through, and lessons from earlier choices—not a generic market presentation.

Year end

Complete the Annual Allocation Review

The Harrison family explains what it owns, why each major allocation still belongs, what changed, what it got wrong, and what should happen next.

The membership rhythm

Useful between meetings.
Disciplined over time.

Between meetings

Questions find experience

Search documented member decisions, manager lessons, and relevant peer experience whenever a new question appears.

Every quarter

Context becomes action

Review what changed, decide what requires attention, and assign clear next steps across the family and its advisers.

Every year

Judgment becomes a record

Decisions, outcomes, mistakes, and lessons accumulate in the Decision Journal and begin forming the family’s Investment Legacy.

By the end of year one

The family has more than organized information.

It has a shared operating picture, trusted relationships, a repeatable decision process, and the beginning of an institutional memory.
